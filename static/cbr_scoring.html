How CBR Scores Are Computed

Each candidate is compared to a set of past candidates whose selection outcome is already known (shortlisted, longlisted, or rejected). The system finds the most similar past candidates, then blends their outcomes — weighted by how closely they match the current applicant — into a single score between 0 and 1.

The Similarity Formula, In Plain Terms

Similarity between two candidates is computed feature-by-feature across four categories. For each feature, a score between 0 and 1 is computed (e.g. 0 = very different, 1 = identical). These feature scores are combined with the category weights you configure to produce an overall similarity score for each pair.

Once the top-k most similar past cases are found, their known labels are averaged — but cases that are more similar count for more. A neighbour with similarity 0.9 has three times the influence of one with similarity 0.3.

Label values: shortlist = 1.0  |  longlist = 0.6  |  reject = 0.2. A score near 1 means the most similar past cases were all shortlisted; near 0.2 means they were mostly rejected.

Education Features

These features describe the candidate's formal academic qualifications.

FeatureWhat it measuresHow similarity is scored
Highest degree completedThe highest academic credential attained (Bachelor, Master, PhD, etc.).Rule-based: PhD → 1.0, Master → 0.85, Bachelor → 0.65. Closer degrees score closer to 1.
GPAOverall academic grade normalised to a 0–100 scale.Proportional: the closer two candidates' GPAs, the higher the similarity.
Thesis gradeMaster's thesis grade, normalised to 0–100.Proportional: same logic as GPA.
Bachelor fieldThe subject area of the bachelor's degree (e.g. Sociology, Geography).Text similarity to the configured preferred / acceptable background lists. Preferred matches score higher than acceptable ones.
Master fieldThe subject area of the master's degree.Same as bachelor field.

Experience Features

These features describe research and professional history.

FeatureWhat it measuresHow similarity is scored
Research yearsYears spent in academic research roles (assistant, fellow, etc.).Proportional up to an 8-year ceiling: the closer two values, the higher the similarity.
Relevant professional yearsYears in roles with clear relevance to the position (policy, industry R&D, etc.).Proportional up to 8 years.
Teaching yearsYears spent in formal teaching or supervision roles.Proportional up to 6 years.
Career gap monthsTotal months of unexplained gaps in the CV.Inverted: longer gaps score lower. Two candidates with equal gaps score 1; large divergence scores 0.
Time since last academic activityMonths since the most recent academic role or output.Inverted: more time away from academia scores lower, up to a 36-month ceiling.

Research Output Features

These features capture scholarly productivity and independent research evidence.

FeatureWhat it measuresHow similarity is scored
Peer-reviewed publicationsNumber of publications in peer-reviewed journals or proceedings.Proportional up to 8. Zero publications scores 0; 8+ scores 1.
First-author publicationsPublications where the candidate is the primary author.Proportional up to 5.
Conference outputsConference papers, posters, or presentations.Proportional up to 8.
Posters or extended abstractsShorter scholarly outputs submitted to academic events.Proportional up to 8.
Independent research outputWhether the candidate has produced any independent output (thesis, working paper, report) beyond coursework.Binary: both have output → 1.0, neither → 1.0, only one does → 0.0.

Skills & Methods Features

These features describe methodological and technical capabilities, and language proficiency.

FeatureWhat it measuresHow similarity is scored
Quantitative methodsWhether the candidate has demonstrated quantitative research skills (statistics, modelling, etc.).Binary: both have / neither has → 1.0; mismatch → 0.0.
Qualitative methodsWhether the candidate has demonstrated qualitative research skills (interviews, discourse analysis, etc.).Binary.
Programming / technical skillsWhether the candidate has programming or technical computing experience.Binary.
Number of programming languagesCount of distinct programming languages the candidate has used.Proportional up to 5.
Experimental / empirical designWhether the candidate has experience designing empirical studies or experiments.Binary.
English levelSelf-reported or inferred English proficiency (basic → fluent → native).Level-based: native → 1.0, fluent → 0.95, C1 → 0.82, B2 → 0.65, etc. Closer levels score closer to 1.
Local language levelProficiency in the working language of the institution (e.g. Norwegian).Same as English level.
