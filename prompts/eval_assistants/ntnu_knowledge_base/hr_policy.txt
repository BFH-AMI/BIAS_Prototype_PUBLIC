NTNU Hiring-Relevant Human Resources Policy

Extracted directly from NTNU's Human Resources Policy

Qualification Principle

The qualification principle is a statutory principle stating that hiring processes must be characterized by:

fairness,
objectivity,
equitable treatment,

so that the person who is best qualified for the position is appointed.

Definition of Recruitment

Recruitment includes the entire process:

identifying competence needs,
advertising positions,
assessing applicants,
hiring,
onboarding,
integration of new employees.
Recruitment and Hiring Goals

NTNU aims to be:

an attractive educational institution,
an internationally recognized research university,
an organization with strong technical and administrative support services.

To achieve this, NTNU seeks to recruit talented employees with:

complementary experience,
complementary skills,
the ability to perform current work assignments,
the ability to meet future needs.
Core Hiring Principles

Public advertisement, transparency about assessment criteria, qualitative assessment and the qualification principle are intended to promote equal opportunities for applicants.

The recruitment process, from strategic assessment of competence needs to appointment, is intended to ensure that NTNU recruits:

"the best qualified candidate based on an overall assessment of the applicants’ qualifications in relation to NTNU’s needs."

Hiring decisions should therefore be based on:

an overall assessment,
applicant qualifications,
NTNU's needs,
qualitative evaluation rather than single indicators.
Equality and Diversity in Hiring

Gender equality and diversity must be included in the needs assessment connected to:

advertising vacancies,
appointments.

NTNU states that:

equality and diversity must be emphasized in recruitment,
equality and diversity must be emphasized in workplace adaptations,
equality and diversity must be emphasized in skills development,
equality and diversity must be emphasized in career development,
equality and diversity must be emphasized in salary decisions.

Respect, equal status, non-discrimination and inclusion should characterize NTNU's organizational culture.

NTNU states that varied experiences and perspectives:

enrich the work environment,
enrich the study environment,
are valuable in problem solving,
are valuable in knowledge development.

As an international research university, NTNU gives high priority to inclusive practices that support integration of international researchers.

Institutional and local measures related to equality and diversity should be adapted to local challenges and needs.

Equal Opportunity Principles

Public advertisement, transparent assessment criteria, qualitative assessment and the qualification principle are intended to promote equal opportunities for applicants.

Hiring processes should therefore be:

transparent,
fair,
objective,
based on clearly communicated criteria,
consistent with equitable treatment of applicants.
Inclusion and Onboarding

During recruitment and follow-up of new employees, NTNU must ensure that employees:

get a good start in their jobs,
are included in the working environment,
become familiar with NTNU's organizational culture,
become familiar with NTNU's values,
become familiar with NTNU's goals.
Permanent and Temporary Employment

All employees should have predictable terms of employment.

NTNU states that:

"Permanent employment provides the best opportunity for long-term planning and for carrying out teaching and research as well as technical and administrative services of high quality."

NTNU will therefore actively work to reduce temporary employment.

However, fixed-term and part-time positions may be used to bring in expertise from:

working life,
academic communities with a strong international reputation.
Accommodation and Accessibility

NTNU aims to be a flexible and inclusive employer.

Adjustments should be made when disabilities, functional needs, stage of life, or age make adjustments desirable or necessary.

The skills and experience of older employees are considered valuable resources for NTNU.

Career Development and Academic Careers

NTNU seeks predictable and equitable career paths.

Career development should allow employees to:

emphasize different competence areas during different career stages,
develop qualifications aligned with their interests,
develop qualifications aligned with the needs of academic communities,
develop qualifications aligned with societal needs.

NTNU recognizes expertise in:

research,
artistic research,
education,
innovation,
dissemination,
external collaboration,
management.

Academic leadership and institutional leadership are recognized as legitimate career paths.

Good academic supervision and early introduction to projects and networks are considered important contributors to high-quality research training and future career development.

International Recruitment

As an international research university, NTNU places high priority on inclusive practices supporting the integration of international researchers.

Relevant External Principles and Frameworks

The human resources policy states that NTNU's recruitment and employment practices are informed by:

The European Charter for Researchers.
The Code of Conduct for the Recruitment of Researchers (Charter & Code).
The Agreement on Reforming Research Assessment (CoARA).
Universities Norway's NOR-CAM toolbox for recognition and rewards in academic careers.
Management Expectations Relevant to Hiring

Managers are expected to:

act fairly,
give recognition for good work,
set boundaries when needed,
exercise leadership with respect and trust in employees' academic integrity.

Mutual respect, openness and clear expectations are described as important for good cooperation and conflict prevention.

Organizational Values Relevant to Evaluation

NTNU's values are:

Creative
Constructive
Critical
Respectful

These values are expected to characterize organizational culture and working relationships.

Academic freedom is identified as a fundamental principle in academic activities.
